## R-001: Prospect-Refuge Balance

**Statement:** Every habitable space must offer a view outward (prospect) from a position that feels sheltered (refuge).

**Intent:** Leverage evolutionary spatial preferences to create spaces that feel safe and engaging without being oppressive.

**Detection:** Does primary seating/work position face outward with back against wall or enclosure? Is there a window or view axis?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Reorient furniture to back walls; add windows on view sides; lower sill heights; add partial ceiling enclosures at seating alcoves.

**Exceptions:** Purely functional spaces (mechanical, storage) where occupancy is incidental.

**Source:** 01_Ch2_Finding a good home (Origins of Architectural Pleasure)

**Confidence:** High

---

## R-002: Proxemic Zone Compliance

**Statement:** Layouts must not force strangers into intimate distance (<18in/45cm) in contexts where personal or social distance is expected.

**Intent:** Prevent involuntary proxemic violations that trigger stress, avoidance, and reduced satisfaction.

**Detection:** Check corridor and waiting area widths. Verify furniture spacing allows personal-distance separation (18in-4ft) in non-intimate contexts.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Widen corridors to >=5ft (1.5m) in high-traffic areas; space seating clusters >=4ft (1.2m) apart in public spaces.

**Exceptions:** Intentionally intimate contexts: nursing, therapy, family dining.

**Source:** 02_ChX_Distances in man_Hidden Dimension

**Confidence:** High

## R-003: Biophilic Minimum Provision

**Statement:** At least two of the 14 biophilic design attributes must be present in any primary occupied space.

**Intent:** Achieve measurable wellbeing benefits (stress reduction, productivity, satisfaction) through minimum viable biophilic provision.

**Detection:** Audit each primary occupied space against Kellert's 14 attributes. Flag spaces with zero biophilic attributes.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Add operable windows, interior plants, natural material finishes, or daylighting to any deficient space.

**Exceptions:** Spaces with no regular occupancy or requiring sterile conditions (clean rooms, procedure rooms).

**Source:** 02_Ch1_Dimensions elements & attributes of biophilic design — 14 attributes

**Confidence:** High

## R-004: Legibility by Lynch Elements

**Statement:** Any building or site plan must contain at minimum one clear path hierarchy, one landmark, and legible zone differentiation between public and private.

**Intent:** Ensure occupant cognitive mapping: a first-time user should form a rough mental model within 2 minutes of entry.

**Detection:** Cognitive walkthrough: can you identify the main path, a landmark, and the public-to-private transition on the floor plan alone?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Introduce a spine corridor as primary path; place landmark (tower, skylight) at main decision node; change material at zone transitions.

**Exceptions:** Single-room or very small-scale spaces below 500 sq ft.

**Source:** 04_Image of the City (Lynch) — 5 elements framework

**Confidence:** High

## R-005: Decision-Point Information Priority

**Statement:** Wayfinding information must be placed at every decision point — intersections, entries, elevator lobbies, and corridor branches.

**Intent:** Reduce wayfinding errors, anxiety, and staff time lost guiding disoriented visitors.

**Detection:** Walk the route from entry to each primary destination. Verify a spatial cue or sign exists at each decision point within line-of-sight.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Add supergraphics, color bands, or overhead signage at each decision point. Ensure destination visibility or directional arrows at every branch.

**Exceptions:** Residential single-family where occupant familiarity substitutes for signage.

**Source:** 05_Ch4_Wayfinding_With people in mind — decision-point principle

**Confidence:** High

## R-006: Healthcare Single-Occupancy Default

**Statement:** Inpatient healthcare facilities must default to single-occupancy patient rooms unless documented programmatic reason exists for shared rooms.

**Intent:** Reduce healthcare-associated infection rates, improve patient sleep and privacy, and support family participation in care.

**Detection:** Review room configuration in healthcare floor plans. Flag any multi-bed rooms and require documented justification.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Convert shared rooms to single-occupancy during design development. If budget prevents, provide full visual privacy curtaining.

**Exceptions:** Specific therapeutic contexts (peer-support rooms) or facility types with evidence for shared occupancy.

**Source:** 07_Ch11_Health by Design — single-occupancy evidence

**Confidence:** High

## R-007: Dementia Loop Circulation

**Statement:** Memory care environments must provide continuous loop walking paths with no dead ends visible from resident living areas.

**Intent:** Reduce elopement attempts, agitation from dead-end wandering, and physical restraint use.

**Detection:** Trace all paths from any resident room: does every path connect back without retracing? Are dead-end corridors visible from common areas?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Redesign corridor geometry to form a loop. Screen dead-ends with furniture or visual barriers. Integrate secure outdoor courtyard with loop.

**Exceptions:** Non-dementia residential; secure acute psychiatric settings with different safety regime.

**Source:** 14_Use of physical environment to support everyday activities for people with dementia

**Confidence:** High

## R-008: Cognitive Load Limit at Decision Nodes

**Statement:** No decision node should present more than 5-7 simultaneous spatial choices or visual elements requiring interpretation.

**Intent:** Respect working memory limits (~7+/-2 items) to prevent spatial overwhelm and disorientation.

**Detection:** At each decision node, count visible path choices + distinct signage elements + competing focal points. Flag if total > 7.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Simplify spatial geometry to reduce branching. Consolidate signage onto one panel per decision point. Use visual hierarchy to suppress secondary information.

**Exceptions:** Transit hubs where high choice-count is intrinsic — compensate with extended approach sequences.

**Source:** 06_Cognition meets Le Corbusier — working memory and spatial complexity

**Confidence:** Medium

## R-009: Passive-First Energy Hierarchy

**Statement:** For any conditioned space, passive strategies (orientation, envelope, natural ventilation, thermal mass) must be optimized before specifying active mechanical systems.

**Intent:** Achieve lower lifecycle carbon by reducing mechanical load rather than compensating for a poor envelope with oversized equipment.

**Detection:** Is WWR >=40% on east/west without shading? Are there thermal bridges in the envelope? Is natural ventilation potential analyzed?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Run passive design alternatives in energy simulation before sizing HVAC. Document the passive design baseline.

**Exceptions:** Healthcare, laboratory, and cleanroom occupancies with stringent ventilation and humidity control requirements.

**Source:** Ching_Green_Building_Illustrated; GZ_Brown_SWL_book — passive-first strategies

**Confidence:** High

## R-010: Solar Orientation Compliance

**Statement:** In temperate northern-hemisphere climates, building long axis must be oriented within +/-15 degrees of true east-west.

**Intent:** Site orientation is the lowest-cost, highest-impact passive sustainability decision — it must be made at project inception.

**Detection:** Check building orientation angle against true north. Flag if long axis is within +/-15 degrees of north-south.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Rotate massing to orient long axis east-west in schematic design. If site constraints prevent, add shading on east and west elevations.

**Exceptions:** Dense urban infill where site geometry constrains orientation; equatorial climate projects.

**Source:** GZ_Brown_SWL_book — orientation chapter; Ching_Green_Building_Illustrated — site section

**Confidence:** High

## R-011: Embodied Carbon Documentation

**Statement:** Specifications for structural systems, envelope, and interior finishes must include embodied carbon data (EPDs or GWP values) for all major materials.

**Intent:** Make embodied carbon a first-class design variable alongside cost and structural performance.

**Detection:** Do structural steel, concrete, insulation, and cladding specs include EPD references or GWP values?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Require EPDs from suppliers as part of specification. Use EC3 or OneClick LCA to benchmark materials against industry averages.

**Exceptions:** Minor finish materials with negligible embodied carbon contribution (paint, small hardware).

**Source:** Ching_Green_Building_Illustrated — materials; ARUP_Two_Degrees_book — embodied carbon

**Confidence:** High

## R-012: Passive Survivability Provision

**Statement:** Buildings in climate zones 1-5 must remain habitable for 72 hours without mechanical systems via natural ventilation, thermal mass, or operable openings.

**Intent:** Address IPCC AR6 locked-in extreme heat events and grid outage scenarios; resilience is a baseline requirement.

**Detection:** Are there operable windows on multiple facades? Is there thermal mass? Can natural cross-ventilation occur without fan assistance?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Add operable window area >=4% of floor area on at least two facades. Increase thermal mass. Verify passive ventilation stack paths.

**Exceptions:** Sealed lab, cleanroom, and data center occupancies where passive operation is structurally incompatible with function.

**Source:** ARUP_Two_Degrees_book — passive survivability; IPCC_AR6_SYR_SPM — extreme heat events

**Confidence:** High

## R-013: ADA Turning Radius at All Activity Nodes

**Statement:** A 5ft x 5ft (1524mm x 1524mm) wheelchair turning radius must be maintained at every activity node, room entry, and direction change in accessible routes.

**Intent:** Ensure genuine ADA compliance beyond corridor width minimums — most failures occur at fixtures, corners, and room entries.

**Detection:** Overlay a 5ft circle at every room entry, toilet room clear floor area, and kitchen work triangle. Flag overlaps with fixed elements.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Increase room dimensions at entry zones. Relocate fixtures to provide clear floor space. Remove door swing conflicts.

**Exceptions:** Non-accessible units in multi-family (per ADA/FHA percentages); maintenance/storage rooms.

**Source:** Architectural_Graphic_Standards_11th — ADA section; The_Architects_Studio_Companion

**Confidence:** High

## R-014: Stair Geometry Compliance

**Statement:** All stairs must comply: riser 7-7.75in, tread >=11in residential; riser <=7in, tread >=11in commercial; rise+run sum = 17-18in.

**Intent:** Ergonomic stair proportions reduce fall risk and fatigue; non-compliant stairs create liability and code violations.

**Detection:** Calculate rise/run ratio for every stair shown. Check against IBC Section 1011 (commercial) or IRC R311 (residential).

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Adjust floor-to-floor height, landing geometry, or run length for compliant proportions.

**Exceptions:** Alternating tread stairs in code-permitted occupancies; ship's ladders in limited-access spaces.

**Source:** Architectural_Graphic_Standards_11th — stair section

**Confidence:** High

## R-015: Structural System Selection by Span

**Statement:** Structural system selection must be validated against span requirements: wood light frame <=40ft; steel/concrete 40-100ft; long-span systems >100ft.

**Intent:** Prevent schematic design decisions that set an uneconomical structural system that must be reversed in design development.

**Detection:** Identify maximum bay span in floor plan. Cross-check against proposed structural system. Flag mismatches.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Use Studio Companion span rules of thumb in schematic design before finalizing column grid. Document structural system rationale.

**Exceptions:** Hybrid systems and architecturally-driven structural expressions — require early structural engineering consultation.

**Source:** The_Architects_Studio_Companion — structure selection

**Confidence:** High

## R-016: Moisture Control in Wood Framing

**Statement:** Wood framing exposed to exterior moisture or ground contact must be pressure-treated or naturally durable species. All framing must be >=6in (150mm) above grade.

**Intent:** Prevent structural decay, mold, and premature failure of wood-framed assemblies.

**Detection:** Does any untreated wood contact concrete, masonry, or soil? Is sill plate pressure-treated? Is frame clearance from grade >=6in?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Specify ACQ or CA pressure-treated lumber for sill plates and all wood within 6in of grade. Detail capillary breaks between concrete and wood.

**Exceptions:** Naturally durable species (western red cedar, black locust) where pressure treatment is impractical.

**Source:** Fundamentals_of_Building_Construction Ch 3 (Wood) — decay resistance

**Confidence:** High

---

## R-017: Rainscreen Cladding Principle

**Statement:** All exterior cladding on wood-framed buildings must be installed over a drained and back-ventilated cavity (minimum 3/8in/10mm) over a continuous WRB.

**Intent:** Eliminate cladding-to-sheathing moisture contact that causes premature failure, mold, and structural damage.

**Detection:** Is there a defined cavity between cladding and WRB? Are weep holes at base flashing shown?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Add 1x4 vertical furring strips over WRB before cladding. Detail open bottom (screened) and open top for convective drying.

**Exceptions:** Fully adhered assemblies (EIFS, direct-applied stucco) require drainage plane, not rainscreen.

**Source:** Fundamentals_of_Building_Construction Ch 6 (Exterior Finishes) — rainscreen principle

**Confidence:** High

---

## R-018: MEP Ceiling Plenum Coordination

**Statement:** A minimum 18in (450mm) floor-to-ceiling plenum must be allocated for MEP coordination in commercial construction with ceiling heights >=9ft finished.

**Intent:** Prevent late-stage coordination conflicts between ductwork, piping, structure, and ceiling systems that result in costly change orders.

**Detection:** Subtract finished ceiling height from floor-to-floor height. If plenum <18in, flag for structural/MEP coordination review.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Increase floor-to-floor height or lower ceiling height to provide >=18in plenum. Use flat-oval ductwork to reduce depth.

**Exceptions:** Exposed structure/ceiling designs where MEP is intentionally exposed; residential with no plenum requirement.

**Source:** Architectural_Graphic_Standards_11th — MEP coordination; The_Architects_Studio_Companion

**Confidence:** High

## R-019: Mass Timber Moisture Protection Protocol

**Statement:** Mass timber elements must be protected from construction moisture during installation and must reach equilibrium MC (<=19%) before enclosure.

**Intent:** Prevent dimensional instability, connection failure, and acoustic performance loss.

**Detection:** Are temporary weather protection requirements specified? Is MC testing at delivery required? Is pre-enclosure MC verification specified?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Add temporary rain protection requirements in general conditions. Specify MC testing at delivery and before enclosure.

**Exceptions:** Exterior/exposed mass timber designed for weathering — use naturally durable species or protected connection details.

**Source:** Fundamentals_of_Building_Construction Ch 4 (Heavy Timber & Mass Timber) — moisture management

**Confidence:** High

## R-020: Elderly Site Accessibility Gradient

**Statement:** Accessible exterior paths serving elderly users must not exceed 1:20 (5%) running slope with rest nodes every 100ft (30m) providing seating at 17-19in height with armrests.

**Intent:** Enable independent outdoor mobility for elderly occupants, with documented benefits for physical health, mood, and social engagement.

**Detection:** Are accessible path slopes verified against 1:20 limit? Are seating nodes spaced <=100ft on all major accessible routes?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Add intermediate ramps or re-grade paths to achieve <=5% slope. Add seating alcoves at 80-100ft intervals on major routes.

**Exceptions:** Emergency egress paths where slope compliance may conflict with grade constraints — document and provide alternative accessible route.

**Source:** 13_Site Planning & Design for elderly — path accessibility

**Confidence:** High

## R-021: Daylighting Depth Rule

**Statement:** Primary work/activity spaces must be positioned within 1.5x the ceiling height distance from a window wall for adequate daylight without artificial supplement.

**Intent:** Reduce artificial lighting energy use and improve occupant circadian entrainment and wellbeing.

**Detection:** Measure room depth from window wall divided by ceiling height. If ratio > 1.5, flag for daylighting analysis.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Add clerestory or toplighting. Use light-shelves to redirect daylight deeper into the plan.

**Exceptions:** Deliberately non-daylit spaces (darkrooms, screening rooms); north-facing high-latitude locations.

**Source:** GZ_Brown_SWL_book — daylighting autonomy; Ching_Green_Building_Illustrated

**Confidence:** High

## R-022: Healthcare EBD Post-Occupancy Requirement

**Statement:** Healthcare projects must specify a post-occupancy evaluation (POE) at 12 months measuring patient satisfaction, staff travel distances, and infection rates vs. baseline.

**Intent:** Close the evidence-based design loop — design decisions cannot be validated without measured outcomes.

**Detection:** Is POE methodology and scope defined? Is funding for POE included in project budget?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Add POE scope to owner-architect agreement. Define metrics at programming phase. Budget 0.5-1% of construction cost for POE activities.

**Exceptions:** Renovation projects where baseline data is unavailable or scope too small for statistically meaningful results.

**Source:** 07_Ch11_Health by Design — EBD; 11_Shepley_Chapter_5 — POE

**Confidence:** Medium

## R-023: Thermal Bridge Elimination at Envelope

**Statement:** Continuous exterior insulation or thermal break must be provided at all framing-to-exterior connections.

**Intent:** Actual energy performance is 20-40% lower than nominal R-value when framing bridges are not addressed; this creates both energy and condensation risks.

**Detection:** Is framing continuous between interior and exterior without an insulation layer interrupting the bridge? Flag every structural connection that penetrates the thermal envelope.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Add continuous exterior rigid insulation (R-5 to R-10 depending on climate zone) over sheathing. Use thermal break clips for cladding attachment.

**Exceptions:** Passive House or net-zero projects address this by specification — verify it is explicitly detailed, not assumed.

**Source:** Fundamentals_of_Building_Construction Ch 6; Ching_Green_Building_Illustrated — envelope

**Confidence:** High

## R-024: Fire Egress Travel Distance Compliance

**Statement:** Travel distance to nearest exit stair from any point in a sprinklered commercial floor must not exceed 200ft (60m); 250ft in assembly occupancies.

**Intent:** Ensure life-safety compliance and avoid costly late-stage stair additions during permit review.

**Detection:** Measure travel distance from the furthest point of each floor to nearest exit stair following actual paths. Flag distances exceeding IBC maximums.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Add exit stair locations. Reduce floor plate depth. Reconfigure interior partitions to shorten travel paths.

**Exceptions:** Per IBC Table 1017.2: specific occupancy types and sprinkler conditions modify limits — confirm with current code.

**Source:** The_Architects_Studio_Companion — fire egress; Architectural_Graphic_Standards_11th

**Confidence:** High

## R-025: Interior Circulation Hierarchy Documentation

**Statement:** Floor plans must demonstrate a three-level circulation hierarchy: primary corridors >=5ft (1.5m), secondary >=3ft (0.9m), tertiary >=2ft (0.6m), each visually distinguishable in plan.

**Intent:** Ensure code-compliant accessible routes, reduce congestion, and clarify spatial hierarchy for occupants.

**Detection:** Measure corridor widths at minimum sections. Identify which corridors are primary, secondary, tertiary. Verify the hierarchy is legible.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Widen primary corridors to >=5ft. Document the circulation hierarchy in a diagram on the floor plan sheet.

**Exceptions:** Single-family residential where tertiary passages (closet access, utility) may be as narrow as 24in.

**Source:** 09_Zhu_Chapter_3 (Space Planning) — circulation hierarchy; Architectural_Graphic_Standards_11th

**Confidence:** High

## R-026: Spatial Zone Count Limit

**Statement:** A building or campus plan must be divided into no more than 5-7 distinct, perceptually coherent zones for wayfinding purposes. Fewer than 3 zones provides insufficient structure; more than 7 overloads memory.

**Intent:** Align zone count with human short-term memory capacity so occupants can form a reliable cognitive map of the whole building on the first visit.

**Detection:** Count the number of perceptually distinct zones (by color, material, function, or spatial enclosure) visible or reachable from the main entry. Flag if count < 3 or > 7.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Merge functionally similar wings into one zone. Add a distinguishing material or lighting cue to differentiate under-differentiated zones. Label zones with simple names visible at entry to each.

**Exceptions:** Transit hubs and airports where zone count necessarily exceeds 7 -- compensate with overhead landmark identifiers and repeated orientation maps.

**Source:** 05_Ch4_Wayfinding_With people in mind_AC -- WF:D1 Regions, p. 3-4

**Confidence:** High

## R-027: Landmark Density and Distinctiveness

**Statement:** Every major path junction must be anchored by one clearly visible, contextually harmonious landmark. Landmark count must not exceed the number of primary path junctions; excessive landmarks cancel each other out.

**Intent:** Landmarks function as cognitive anchors allowing occupants to determine location and direction without signage. Overloading degrades this function.

**Detection:** Identify every primary path intersection. Verify one distinguishable landmark exists within 30 ft of each. Count total landmarks -- flag if total > (number of primary junctions + 2).

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Select one element per junction to serve as the landmark. Remove competing elements at the same junction. Ensure landmark is visible from >=20 ft approach distance.

**Exceptions:** Single-corridor buildings with one entry/exit and no path branching.

**Source:** 05_Ch4_Wayfinding_With people in mind_AC -- WF:D2 Landmarks, p. 5-6

**Confidence:** High

## R-028: Posted Map Orientation Alignment

**Statement:** All fixed You-Are-Here (YAH) maps must be oriented so that the viewer's forward direction corresponds to the map's up direction. North-up convention must not be used for posted wayfinding maps.

**Intent:** Viewer-aligned maps eliminate the mental rotation step that causes systematic errors; north-up posted maps consistently confuse and misdirect users (documented effect: Levine et al. 1982).

**Detection:** Review all YAH map locations. Verify that the map's upper half depicts the area ahead of the viewer's natural standing position at each posting location.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Generate a separate map rendering for each posting location rotated to match viewer direction. Provide a small north arrow for reference only -- not as the primary orientation cue.

**Exceptions:** Downloadable/printable maps where viewer orientation is unknown at print time.

**Source:** 05_Ch4_Wayfinding_With people in mind_AC -- WF:M4, p. 17; 06_Cognition meets Le Corbusier -- YAH alignment effect, p. 4

**Confidence:** High

## R-029: New-Visitor Map Minimalism

**Statement:** Wayfinding maps provided to new visitors must contain exactly three categories of information: (1) landmarks, (2) path choice points, and (3) major zone regions. All other information must be omitted.

**Intent:** Excess information on wayfinding maps actively undermines usability by cognitively overwhelming new visitors and may cause them to avoid the map entirely.

**Detection:** Audit each map for information categories. Flag maps with >3 symbol types or >10 labeled elements per floor.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Create a new-visitor layer stripped to landmarks, choice points, and zone labels. Reserve full-detail maps for facility staff. Test both versions with unfamiliar users before deployment.

**Exceptions:** Regulatory safety maps (egress, AED) with mandatory content -- provide as a separate document.

**Source:** 05_Ch4_Wayfinding_With people in mind_AC -- WF:M1 Orientation for the new visitor, p. 9-11

**Confidence:** High

## R-030: Orthogonal Reference Frame Alignment

**Statement:** All primary occupied zones must be laid out on a consistent orthogonal grid. Diagonal or rotated plan geometries are permitted only in secondary or transitional spaces and must be flagged for wayfinding review.

**Intent:** Orthogonal layouts allow occupants to build accurate room-to-room spatial maps using the same mental reference axes throughout the building. Non-orthogonal sections force reference-frame re-registration, multiplying navigation errors.

**Detection:** Overlay a grid aligned to the building's main entry axis. Identify any room or wing whose primary axes deviate >15 degrees from this grid. Flag all deviations in primary occupied zones.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Rotate deviant wings to align with the primary grid, or introduce a transition space (lobby, atrium) that clearly signals the reference-frame shift.

**Exceptions:** Deliberately expressive architectural geometries in secondary spaces -- require explicit wayfinding mitigation documentation.

**Source:** 06_Cognition meets Le Corbusier -- orthogonal room memory advantage, p. 5-6; Gottingen Rathaus example, p. 11

**Confidence:** High

## R-031: Floor Plan Interconnection Density Limit

**Statement:** Floor plan Interconnection Density (ICD, O'Neill 1991) -- the mean number of paths from any decision node -- must not exceed 1.50 for buildings occupied by non-expert users. ICD >1.50 predicts measurably increased wayfinding errors.

**Intent:** Provides a computable geometric metric for navigational complexity that can be checked algorithmically on a floor plan graph before construction.

**Detection:** Build a graph of all decision nodes and connecting paths. Compute mean path options per node. Flag plans with ICD >1.50 for healthcare, education, civic, and residential occupancy types.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Reduce branching by simplifying corridor geometry. Convert T-intersections to dead-end side rooms where functionally possible. Add linear spine corridors to reduce mean ICD.

**Exceptions:** Transit facilities and shopping centers where high ICD is intrinsic -- compensate with continuous overhead zone identification and redundant signage.

**Source:** 06_Cognition meets Le Corbusier -- ICD index (O'Neill 1991), p. 7-8, Fig. 2

**Confidence:** Medium

## R-032: Entry Sequence Primacy

**Statement:** The first clear view from a building's primary entry must communicate: (a) the building's main organizational axis, (b) the primary destination direction, and (c) at least one major zone boundary -- all within 30 ft of the entry threshold.

**Intent:** First-view primacy is a documented cognitive effect -- the reference system formed at entry persists and governs error-correction throughout the visit.

**Detection:** From the primary entry threshold, verify all three elements are visible: main corridor direction, primary destination, and one zone transition. Flag any missing element.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Redesign entry vestibule to open onto the main spine corridor. Place primary destination landmark in the direct sightline from entry. Use material change to mark zone transition at entry.

**Exceptions:** Security-controlled entries where visibility is intentionally restricted -- substitute with mandatory escort or visible wayfinding kiosk at entry.

**Source:** 06_Cognition meets Le Corbusier -- first-view primacy (Shelton & McNamara 1997), p. 4-5

## R-033: Local Reference Frame Consistency

**Statement:** No structural or spatial element (elevator core, service wing, rotated pavilion) may be oriented at an angle to the building's primary axis grid without a visible, occupant-legible transition element that signals the geometric shift.

**Intent:** Subsections rotated relative to the main grid create invisible reference-frame conflicts -- spaces perceived as squares are actually diamonds relative to the building, causing systematic spatial errors for occupants navigating between zones.

**Detection:** Identify any floor plan element whose interior walls are not parallel to the building's primary grid. Verify a visible transition element marks the boundary. Flag unmarked angular transitions.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Where rotation is required, add a transition lobby whose geometry explicitly mediates between the two grids. Landmark the transition point. Test with cognitive walkthrough.

**Exceptions:** Deliberately expressive structural geometry where disorientation is an intended architectural experience -- document wayfinding mitigation explicitly.

## R-034: Envelope Layer Continuity

**Statement:** The building's thermal and air barrier layers must be continuous and unbroken across all planes -- walls, roofs, floors, and penetrations. Every discontinuity (gap, thermal bridge, unsealed penetration) must be shown in detail drawings before enclosure.

**Intent:** Envelope discontinuities can negate 20-40% of a wall's nominal R-value and cause chronic moisture problems that are expensive to remediate after enclosure.

**Detection:** Trace the continuous thermal boundary on a building section from below grade to roof. Identify every interruption: structural penetrations, electrical boxes, ducts, window frames, framing members. Flag all undetailed penetrations.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Require air barrier detailing at all penetrations as a drawing requirement. Specify blower-door test at pre-insulation rough-in. Use continuous rigid exterior insulation to eliminate framing thermal bridges.

**Exceptions:** Intentionally ventilated assemblies (vented rainscreen cavities, unconditioned attics) where the air barrier plane is explicitly located at the appropriate layer by design.

**Source:** Ching_Green_Building_Illustrated -- Continuity, thermal bridges, Section 2.07-2.08, p. 19-20

**Confidence:** High

## R-035: Holistic Green Performance Check

**Statement:** No building may be claimed as green or sustainable based on a single high-performance component without demonstrating that energy, water, envelope, and IAQ are all addressed as a whole system.

**Intent:** Single-feature greenwashing is a documented failure mode -- a building with one visible green element but poor integrated performance can use more energy than a code-minimum non-green building.

**Detection:** Audit building for all major loss pathways: envelope U-value, air leakage rate, lighting power density, HVAC efficiency, water use intensity. Flag any sustainability claim where any pathway performs below code minimum.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Require whole-building energy model before issuing any sustainability certification or marketing claim. Apply weakest-link heuristic: the worst-performing system defines overall performance.

**Exceptions:** Partial renovation projects where scope is limited by existing conditions -- scope limitations must be disclosed in all sustainability claims.

**Source:** Ching_Green_Building_Illustrated -- Holistic design, Section 2.11, p. 21

**Confidence:** High

## R-036: Exterior Door Count Minimization

**Statement:** Building envelope design must minimize the count of exterior door assemblies. For multi-unit residential, interior corridor access is preferred over individual unit exterior doors.

**Intent:** Doors degrade faster than walls -- warped frames, failed weatherstripping, and compressed door sweeps are chronic infiltration sources. Minimizing count is more effective long-term than specifying better hardware.

**Detection:** Count total exterior door assemblies. For multi-unit residential: flag individual unit exterior doors where interior corridor access is feasible. For commercial: flag exterior door count >1 per 5,000 sq ft without vestibule.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Switch to interior corridor access for multi-unit residential. Consolidate loading and service entries. Specify airlock vestibule at all remaining exterior doors in climate zones 4-8.

**Exceptions:** Life-safety egress requirements mandate minimum door counts and may not be reduced to meet this rule.

**Source:** Ching_Green_Building_Illustrated -- Nonrobust layers and exterior door count, Section 2.09-2.10, p. 20-21

## R-037: Outside-In Design Sequence

**Statement:** Green design decisions must follow the outside-in sequence: community, site, building shape, near-building features, outer envelope, unconditioned buffer spaces, inner envelope, thermal zoning, MEP systems. Later-sequence systems may not compensate for failures in earlier-sequence layers.

**Intent:** Each outer layer reduces the load the next layer must handle. Over-sizing mechanical systems to compensate for a poor envelope creates a building whose performance degrades when systems age or fail.

**Detection:** In schematic design, verify passive site and shape strategies are documented before mechanical systems are specified. Flag any project where HVAC capacity is >=20% above what an optimized passive baseline would require.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Run passive design alternatives in early SD and document the baseline before MEP design begins. Require this documentation as a gate before schematic design is approved.

**Exceptions:** Occupancy types with mandatory mechanical requirements (hospitals, labs, data centers) -- document the mandatory active load and design the envelope to minimize everything else.

**Source:** Ching_Green_Building_Illustrated -- Outside-in sequence Section 2.06, p. 18; Holistic design Section 2.11, p. 21

## R-038: Foundation Type by Soil Bearing Capacity

**Statement:** Foundation system selection must be matched to site-specific soil bearing capacity confirmed by geotechnical investigation: spread footings for competent soils (>=1,500 psf); mat/raft for variable or compressible soils; piles or piers where bearing capacity is inadequate at grade.

**Intent:** Foundation selection based on assumed rather than tested soil conditions is a leading cause of differential settlement, structural cracking, and costly post-construction remediation.

**Detection:** Is there a geotechnical report on file? Does the structural foundation type match the report's recommended bearing strategy? Flag any project where foundation type is specified before geotechnical investigation is complete.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Make geotechnical investigation a required pre-design task. Do not finalize foundation type until the structural engineer has reviewed the geotechnical report.

**Exceptions:** Small-scale additions on well-documented soils where prior investigations are on record and conditions are verified unchanged.

## R-039: Seismic Design Category Confirmation

**Statement:** Seismic Design Category (SDC) per IBC must be determined from mapped spectral response values and occupancy category at project initiation. Structural system type, detailing requirements, and height limits all depend on SDC and must not be assumed.

**Intent:** Late discovery of SDC forces structural redesign, cost overruns, and schedule delays. Seismic detailing requirements vary dramatically by category -- a building detailed for SDC B and built in SDC D will be severely under-detailed.

**Detection:** Has the project team confirmed SDC from current USGS hazard maps and the applicable IBC edition? Is SDC documented in the structural design criteria? Flag any project where SDC is not confirmed before structural system selection.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Require SDC confirmation as a structural design criteria deliverable at SD phase kickoff. Use USGS online hazard mapper for preliminary confirmation.

**Exceptions:** Non-structural interior renovations in existing structures where the lateral system is unchanged.

## R-040: Floor System Span-Depth Validation

**Intent:** Span decisions that exceed a system's range discovered in design development require structural redesign -- the later the discovery, the more costly the fix.

## R-041: Climate Zone Wall Section Compliance

**Statement:** Exterior wall assemblies must match the climate-zone-specific section family: hot-arid (vented-skin masonry or radiant barrier); hot-humid (exterior vapor retarder, vented cladding); cold (continuous exterior insulation, interior vapor retarder). Wrong vapor retarder placement causes chronic moisture damage within 5-10 years.

**Intent:** Climate-inappropriate wall sections are a leading cause of building envelope failure -- the error is invisible at construction completion but produces mold, structural decay, and costly remediation within a building's first decade.

**Detection:** Identify project climate zone (ASHRAE 90.1 / IECC zone). Verify wall section assembly type matches the appropriate zone family. Check vapor retarder location: interior for cold climates, exterior for hot-humid, wind barrier only for hot-arid.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Use AGS climate wall section figures (Section 1.6-1.8) as the baseline for each zone. Require envelope consultant review for any project in mixed or ambiguous climate zones.

**Exceptions:** Fully mechanically controlled environments (cleanrooms, cold storage) where climate-specific assembly logic does not apply -- follow manufacturer's specifications.

## R-042: Path Hierarchy Visual Differentiation

**Statement:** Every building or site plan must distinguish primary paths from secondary paths through at least two perceptual cues: width, material, lighting level, or ceiling height.

## R-043: Edge Design Intent Declaration

**Statement:** Every linear element that functions as a spatial edge must be explicitly designated in design documents as either a seam (connecting two districts) or a barrier (dividing them), with appropriate design treatment for each.

**Intent:** Unintentional edges -- service corridors, mechanical chases, parking structures -- routinely break urban and interior spatial continuity without designers recognizing them as edges. Naming them forces a design decision.

## R-044: District Perceptual Homogeneity

**Statement:** Each programmatic district in a building or site must be legible as a zone through at least one consistent perceptual attribute: common floor material, wall color family, ceiling height, or use pattern visible from circulation.

## R-045: Landmark Positional Uniqueness

**Statement:** Landmarks must be placed at decision nodes (intersections, entry points, floor-transition zones). A visually prominent element that is not at a decision node does not qualify as a wayfinding landmark.

**Intent:** Lynch demonstrates that positional uniqueness -- occupying a location that can only be one place -- is as important as visual prominence for landmark effectiveness. A distinctive element mid-corridor anchors no decision.

## R-046: Social-Distance Workstation Compliance

**Statement:** Open-office workstation spacing must provide a minimum 4ft (1.2m) desk-to-visitor clear distance in the primary interaction axis. Workstations that cannot achieve this must have a designated private meeting point within 30ft.

**Exceptions:** Collaborative studio environments (design studios, labs) where proximity is a program requirement and occupants self-select into the space.

## R-047: Semi-Fixed Furniture Adaptability

**Statement:** All shared occupant spaces must include at least 20% of furniture that is readily reconfigurable by occupants without tools or facility management. Fixed or heavy furniture counts do not satisfy this requirement.

## R-048: Acoustic-Visual Privacy Parity

**Statement:** Any space designed to provide visual privacy (partition, office enclosure, screened area) must also achieve equivalent acoustic privacy. Visual privacy without acoustic privacy does not satisfy proxemic separation requirements.

**Intent:** Hall documents that acoustic penetration is as proxemically invasive as visual exposure -- overheard conversations trigger the same involuntary proximity stress as unwanted eye contact. ## R-049: Collaborative Seating Orientation

**Statement:** Seating layouts in collaborative and social spaces must use angled or circular arrangements. Row (theater/classroom) seating is prohibited in rooms designated for informal conversation or social interaction.

**Intent:** Hall cites research that face-to-face and angled arrangements increase conversational engagement while parallel row seating actively suppresses it. Applying row seating to social rooms produces spaces that look occupied but fail to generate intended social interaction.

## R-050: Patient Room Nature View Provision

## R-051: Hospital Noise Level Standard

**Statement:** Patient room background noise must not exceed WHO guidelines (35 dB daytime / 30 dB nighttime). Mechanical, structural, and operational noise sources must all be documented and addressed in the acoustic design narrative.

## R-052: Decentralized Nursing Station Proximity

## R-061: Optimal Complexity Threshold

**Statement:** Interior surface complexity must target an intermediate level: no uninterrupted expanse of a single material, texture, or color exceeding 200 sq ft without variation; and no surface with more than 4 simultaneous competing visual patterns within a single 8 ft x 8 ft field.

**Intent:** Berlyne's inverted-U model shows that both under-complexity (blank, monotonous) and over-complexity (chaotic, overloaded) produce negative affect. Intermediate complexity maximizes occupant preference and wellbeing.

**Detection:** Identify wall or floor surfaces exceeding 200 sq ft of a single unvaried material. Also identify any <=64 sq ft surface with more than 4 competing patterns. Flag both extremes.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Break large monolithic surfaces with texture bands, material transitions, or art placement. Simplify over-patterned surfaces by replacing subordinate patterns with plain variants of the dominant material.

**Exceptions:** Clean-room and clinical procedure areas where surface uniformity is an infection-control or functional requirement.

**Source:** 01_Ch2_Finding a good home_Origins_of_Architectural_Pleasure_AC -- Berlyne optimal complexity, p. 8-10

**Confidence:** Medium

## R-062: Spatial Coherence Requirement

**Statement:** Each room or spatial zone must exhibit coherence: all primary surfaces (floor, dominant wall, ceiling) must share a common organizing principle -- material family, color temperature, or geometric motif.

**Intent:** Coherence is a prerequisite for rapid spatial parsing. Without it, occupants expend cognitive resources forming a room schema rather than attending to activities. Incoherent spaces consistently produce lower comfort and satisfaction ratings even when individual elements are high quality.

**Detection:** For each primary space, identify the organizing principle. Verify that floor, dominant wall, and ceiling are all legibly related to it. Flag spaces where the three primary surfaces have no discernible common language.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Select a single material family or color temperature as the room's organizing primitive, then select all other finishes as variations on it.

**Exceptions:** Deliberately eclectic programs (entertainment, arts, retail) where incoherence is explicit design intent -- document the intent.

## R-063: Affordance Legibility

**Statement:** Every primary user action a space supports (sit, eat, work, collaborate, circulate) must be communicated by physical form without reliance on signage. Any activity whose affordance requires a sign to communicate must be redesigned.

**Intent:** Gibson's affordance theory holds that spaces invite behaviors through their form. Spaces that require signage to explain their use are under-designed. Good design makes affordances immediately readable from physical dimensions, heights, and materials.

**Detection:** Conduct a cognitive walkthrough. For each programmed activity, ask: is it physically obvious from the form that this activity is intended here? Flag any activity whose affordance requires a sign.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Adjust dimensions, heights, or materials to make the affordance explicit. Remove signage compensating for unclear form and redesign the form instead.

**Exceptions:** Safety-critical instructions (egress, emergency) where signage is mandated regardless of affordance clarity.

**Source:** 01_Ch2_Finding a good home_Origins_of_Architectural_Pleasure_AC -- Gibson affordance theory, p. 14-16

## R-064: Enclosure-Openness Gradient

**Statement:** Every inhabited floor of a building must include both enclosed refuge zones (ceiling height <=9 ft, enclosure on >=3 sides) and open prospect zones (ceiling height >=12 ft or unobstructed view >=30 ft). At least 15% of occupied floor area must be of each type.

**Intent:** Evolutionary spatial psychology demonstrates that neither pure openness nor pure enclosure is optimal. Buildings with only open plans or only cellular offices consistently produce lower wellbeing ratings. Alternating between refuge and prospect matches the human preference range.

**Detection:** Map all spaces by ceiling height and enclosure level. Verify at least 15% of occupied floor area is enclosed refuge type and 15% is open prospect type. Flag floors >85% one type.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Add low-ceiling alcoves or enclosed collaboration rooms to open-plan floors. Add an atrium, raised ceiling zone, or view corridor to cellular floor plans.

**Exceptions:** Specialized occupancies (surgery suites, data centers) where spatial type is determined by function.

**Source:** 01_Ch2_Finding a good home_Origins_of_Architectural_Pleasure_AC -- enclosure/openness balance, p. 6-8

**Confidence:** Medium

## R-065: Thermal and Airflow Variability

**Statement:** Occupied zones must not maintain a single static temperature 100% of operating hours. HVAC controls must allow a >=2 degrees F temperature drift and a perceptible airflow variation (still to 0.2 m/s) within occupied zones over the course of a day.

**Intent:** Constant HVAC uniformity departs from the variable thermal environment in which human thermoregulation evolved. Slight thermal and airflow variation improves alertness, thermal satisfaction, and perceived air quality compared to constant setpoints.

**Detection:** Review HVAC control strategy. Verify static setpoints are not held +/-0.5 degrees F continuously. Confirm at least one zone has natural ventilation, operable windows, ceiling fans, or a programmed temperature drift mode.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Program HVAC setpoints with a drift schedule. Add ceiling fans to provide low-velocity airflow variation. Introduce operable windows or vents in at least one zone per floor.

**Exceptions:** Clean rooms, operating suites, and environments where tight temperature control is a regulatory requirement.

**Source:** 02_Ch1_Dimensions elements & attributes of biophilic design_AC -- thermal/airflow variability, p. 6-8

## R-066: Dynamic Daylight Priority

**Statement:** Primary occupied zones must receive natural light that visibly changes in direction or intensity over the day. Dynamic natural light must be the primary lighting source for >=50% of occupied daytime hours. Windowless spaces or zones with no perceptible daily light change do not meet this requirement.

**Intent:** Biophilic design research documents that changing, diffuse natural light -- moving shadows, shifting color temperature -- produces measurably higher wellbeing and alertness than static artificial lighting at equivalent illuminance levels.

**Detection:** Model annual sun angles. Verify >=50% of the primary occupied zone receives daylight that visibly shifts between 9 AM and 3 PM. Flag zones relying entirely on static artificial light during occupied hours.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Add clerestories or skylights with a sun-path offset to produce moving shadow patterns. Specify artificial lighting systems that dim and shift color temperature to supplement natural light patterns.

**Exceptions:** Below-grade and interior-core spaces where direct daylight access is infeasible -- require circadian-tunable artificial lighting as mitigation.

## R-067: Seasonal and Weather Legibility

## R-068: Fractal Geometry at Human Scale

## R-069: Multi-Scale Biophilic Integration

## R-070: Elderly Path Grade Maximum

**Statement:** Exterior paths in senior living and aging-in-place environments must not exceed 1:20 (5%) running slope or 1:50 (2%) cross slope. Rest nodes with seating (see R-071) must be provided at intervals of no more than 150 ft along every exterior path.

**Intent:** Older adults with reduced lower-limb strength and altered gait experience falls and fatigue at grades younger adults navigate easily. The 1:20/1:50 thresholds and 150 ft interval represent evidence-based maximums for independent outdoor mobility in the elderly.

**Detection:** Survey all exterior path grades. Verify running slope <=1:20 and cross slope <=1:50. Measure path lengths between rest nodes and flag any gap >150 ft.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Re-grade paths to comply, introducing switchbacks rather than steeper direct routes on sloped sites. Insert rest nodes with compliant seating every 150 ft maximum.

**Exceptions:** Emergency access routes governed by fire-code minimum grades -- provide an alternative accessible path.

**Source:** 13_Site Planning & Design for elderly_AC -- maximum path slopes, rest node intervals, p. 3-5

**Confidence:** High

## R-071: Elderly Outdoor Seating Standard

**Statement:** Outdoor seating in senior living environments must be: (a) 17-19 in seat height, (b) equipped with armrests extending to the seat front for rising assistance, (c) shaded or sheltered within 20 ft, and (d) visible from at least one indoor common area. Seating not meeting all four criteria does not count toward the rest-node interval requirement in R-070.

**Intent:** Standard bench heights (14-16 in) require quadriceps and hip-flexor strength that declines with age. Without armrests, rising is a documented fall risk. Shade and indoor visibility improve actual seating use.

**Detection:** Audit all outdoor seating. Measure seat height; verify armrests extend to seat front; confirm shade/shelter within 20 ft; verify sightline from at least one interior window. Flag any seat that fails on any criterion.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Replace non-compliant benches with age-specific seating. Add shade structures or plant shade trees at exposed seating nodes. Orient seating toward interior common areas for mutual visibility.

**Exceptions:** Seating in non-assisted-living zones of a mixed-use site -- standard bench specifications apply in those zones.

**Source:** 13_Site Planning & Design for elderly_AC -- bench height, armrests, shade, visibility, p. 5-8

**Confidence:** High

## R-072: Elderly Site Lighting Minimum

**Statement:** All exterior paths and entries in senior living environments must achieve a minimum 10 footcandles (fc) at grade for nighttime safety. Lighting transitions between zones must not produce a contrast ratio greater than 5:1 within a single step.

**Intent:** Older adults have reduced dark adaptation and are more susceptible to disorienting glare transitions. Falls at exterior thresholds are a leading injury cause in the elderly. Even illumination and controlled contrast ratios are documented fall-risk reducers.

**Detection:** Measure footcandle levels at grade at the darkest point between light fixtures. Flag any point below 10 fc. Measure luminance at zone transitions and flag contrast >5:1.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Add intermediate fixtures to eliminate dark zones. Specify full cut-off fixtures to eliminate glare. Install transition zones with graduated luminance at building entry thresholds.

**Exceptions:** Dark-sky preserves or rural sites with lighting restrictions -- use path-edge ground lighting at 0.5 fc minimum with reflective pavement markings as a documented alternative.

**Source:** 13_Site Planning & Design for elderly_AC -- 10 fc minimum, glare transition control, p. 8-10

## R-073: Elderly Wayfinding Contrast Standard

## R-074: Elderly Fall Prevention Surface

**Statement:** All exterior paving in senior living environments must be: (a) slip-resistant with wet dynamic coefficient of friction (DCOF) >=0.42, (b) flush with no level changes >1/4 in, (c) free of open joints >1/2 in wide, and (d) even and stable (no rocking pavers). All ramps and stairs must have continuous handrails on both sides.

**Intent:** Surface irregularities, level changes, and slippery wet finishes are the primary physical causes of outdoor falls in the elderly. Each criterion addresses a documented fall mechanism: trips (level changes), tip hazards (open joints), instability (rocking pavers), and slip (low DCOF).

**Detection:** Walk all exterior paving with a level. Verify DCOF from material specifications or field test. Identify any level change >1/4 in, joint >1/2 in, or rocking element. Verify handrails on both sides of all ramps and stairs.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Re-set or replace non-compliant paving. Specify replacement materials by DCOF rating. Install edge restraints to prevent paver movement. Add handrails where missing.

**Exceptions:** Grass, gravel, and decomposed granite areas designated as non-accessible zones -- provide alternative compliant routes.

## R-075: Dementia Exit Visibility Reduction

**Statement:** In dementia care environments, exits not intended for unsupervised resident use must not be visually prominent from primary resident circulation areas. Door hardware, door color, and door frame must be specified to minimize visual salience using approved non-restraint camouflage methods.

**Intent:** Elopement is a leading safety incident in dementia care. Reducing the visual prominence of non-intended exits significantly decreases elopement attempts without physical restraints, preserving resident dignity and autonomy.

## R-077: Dementia Circadian Lighting Protocol

**Statement:** All residential and common areas in dementia care facilities must be equipped with tunable lighting delivering >=300 lux at seated resident eye level during daytime hours and transitioning to <=50 lux warm-spectrum light in the evening. Circadian programs must be operational from opening day.

**Intent:** People with dementia frequently experience disrupted circadian rhythms. High-intensity cool-spectrum daytime lighting and low-intensity warm evening lighting are documented to improve sleep quality, reduce agitation, and slow cognitive decline.

**Detection:** Measure illuminance at seated eye level (~40 in above floor) during peak daytime hours. Verify fixtures support color temperature tuning. Confirm a written circadian lighting program exists and is programmed into the control system.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Specify LED tunable-white fixtures throughout residential and common areas. Program BAS with a circadian curve: 5000-6500K at 300+ lux from 8 AM-4 PM, transitioning to 2700-3000K at <=50 lux by 8 PM.

**Exceptions:** Procedure, medication, and staff-only areas where task lighting requirements override circadian programming.

## R-078: Dementia Room Personalization Provision

## R-079: Dementia Reverberation Control

**Statement:** All residential, dining, and common areas in dementia care facilities must achieve a reverberation time (RT60) of <=0.6 seconds at 500 Hz. Hard-surfaced rooms exceeding this target must add sound-absorptive treatment before occupancy.

**Intent:** People with dementia have impaired auditory processing and reduced ability to filter reverberation from direct speech. High RT60 causes disorientation, impairs communication with care staff, and is a documented trigger for agitation episodes -- separate from absolute noise level.

**Exceptions:** Corridors and transitional spaces where RT60 <=0.8 s is acceptable; staff-only areas without resident occupancy.

## R-088: Acoustic Noise Control Hierarchy

**Statement:** Acoustic design must address noise in this priority sequence: (1) source avoidance -- site away from noisy sources, specify quiet equipment; (2) absorption -- high-NRC furnishings and finishes; (3) blocking -- solid walls and isolation; (4) masking -- last resort only.

**Intent:** Noise causes auditory harm and non-auditory health effects (cardiovascular, sleep, cognitive). Addressing noise at the source is always more effective and less expensive than downstream mitigation.

**Detection:** For each noise-sensitive space, document which of the four tiers have been applied. Masking-only solutions are non-compliant unless tiers 1-3 were demonstrably infeasible.

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** At SD, locate noise-sensitive spaces away from mechanical rooms and traffic. At DD, specify NRC values for all ceiling and wall materials. At CD, document isolation assembly STC ratings.

**Exceptions:** Spaces where ambient noise levels are inherently low; masking may be applied as a comfort enhancement.

## R-090: Nature Contact Type Diversification

**Statement:** Biophilic design must provide at least three distinct modes of nature contact: passive viewing (window views, artwork, natural materials), sensory presence (plants, water, natural light), and active interaction (garden, courtyard, rooftop access).

## R-111: Heating-Dominated Climate Passive Strategy Hierarchy

**Statement:** In heating-dominated climates (HDD > 5,000), the passive design strategy must follow the hierarchy: compact building form to minimise surface-to-volume ratio, high insulation and air-sealing, south-facing glazing with thermal mass. Active heating systems are sized after passive load reduction is maximised.

**Intent:** In heating-dominated climates, envelope heat loss dominates energy use. Compact form reduces surface area; high insulation and south glazing with thermal mass are the most cost-effective passive strategies per unit of energy saved.

**Detection:** Is the building form compact (surface-to-volume ratio benchmarked against a simple rectangular reference)? Is glazing concentrated on the south facade? Is thermal mass specified adjacent to south glazing?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** During massing studies, calculate surface-to-volume ratio for all form options and select the most compact feasible scheme; locate primary glazing south ±15°; specify thermal mass at 4–6 in thickness adjacent to south-facing glass.

**Exceptions:** Mixed-climate or cooling-dominated sites; buildings with large internal heat gains that offset heating loads.

**Source:** GZ_Brown_SWL_book.pdf (Heating Strategies / compact form, insulation, south glazing)

**Confidence:** High

## R-112: Cooling-Dominated Climate Strategy Hierarchy

**Statement:** In cooling-dominated climates (CDD > 2,000), passive cooling strategies must be applied in sequence: shading of all glazing first, natural ventilation second, earth coupling or evaporative cooling third. Mechanical cooling is sized only after passive strategies are maximised.

**Intent:** Shading is the single highest-leverage strategy in cooling climates; unshaded glazing generates heat gain that active systems cannot cost-effectively overcome. Natural ventilation and earth coupling add free cooling before mechanical systems are engaged.

**Detection:** Are all glazed surfaces shaded for peak summer sun angles? Is natural ventilation documented as feasible for the climate? Is mechanical cooling plant sized after passive load reduction is applied?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Size overhangs and shading devices for the design solar angle before mechanical system sizing; run natural ventilation feasibility analysis (wind pressure and thermal buoyancy) before defaulting to mechanical cooling.

**Exceptions:** Buildings with high internal heat gain densities (data centres, labs) where passive cooling is insufficient regardless of envelope strategy.

## R-113: Solar Overhang Design — Solstice Cut-off Rule

**Statement:** Fixed horizontal overhangs on south-facing glazing (northern hemisphere) must be sized to shade the full glazing area at solar noon on the summer solstice and to admit direct sun at solar noon on the winter solstice. Intermediate seasons are used to calibrate the shading fraction.

**Intent:** This rule of thumb, derived from Brown & DeKay's sun angle geometry, ensures that fixed overhangs provide cooling benefit in summer without sacrificing solar heat gain in winter — the fundamental passive solar balance.

**Detection:** For each south-facing glazed opening, has the overhang depth been calculated using the summer and winter solstice noon sun angle for the building's latitude? Does the shadow line reach the sill at summer solstice noon?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Use the overhang geometry formula: depth = window height / (tan(summer solstice altitude) − tan(winter solstice altitude)); verify with a sun path diagram for the project latitude.

**Exceptions:** Facades with external obstruction (adjacent buildings) that already provide shading; facades on non-south orientations where different shading geometry applies.

**Source:** GZ_Brown_SWL_book.pdf (Solar Control / overhang geometry)

**Confidence:** High

## R-114: Natural Ventilation Opening Configuration

**Statement:** For cross-ventilation, operable openings must be provided on opposite or adjacent building faces relative to the prevailing wind direction, with combined free area ≥ 4% of the floor area served. For stack ventilation, inlet openings must be at a lower level and exhaust at a higher level, with a minimum vertical separation of 3 m.

**Intent:** Cross-ventilation requires a pressure differential created by wind; openings on the same face reduce differential. **Detection:** Are cross-ventilation openings on opposite or adjacent building faces? Is the combined free area of openings ≥ 4% of floor area? For stack systems, is there ≥ 3 m vertical separation between inlet and outlet?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** During SD window placement, confirm opposite-face openings for cross-ventilation; for stack configurations, locate atria or clerestory exhaust openings ≥ 3 m above inlet level.

**Exceptions:** Sealed buildings with stringent acoustic or air quality requirements where natural ventilation is not feasible.

## R-116: Passive Solar Thermal Mass Sizing

**Statement:** In passive solar heating designs, provide thermal mass at a ratio of 30–40 BTU/°F of thermal mass heat capacity per square foot of south glazing. Mass must be directly sunlit or within the same thermal zone as the glazing; insulated or shaded mass does not count toward this ratio.

**Intent:** Undersized thermal mass relative to south glazing area causes overheating on sunny days and poor heat retention at night — the classic passive solar oscillation problem. Brown & DeKay's ratio is derived from empirical testing of direct-gain passive solar buildings.

**Detection:** Has the thermal mass heat capacity been calculated (mass × specific heat × ΔT) and verified against the 30–40 BTU/°F per ft² of south glazing benchmark? Is the mass directly sunlit?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Calculate thermal mass capacity for all direct-gain elements (concrete slab, masonry walls, tile); if below the 30 BTU/°F threshold, add a phase-change material layer or increase slab thickness.

**Exceptions:** Indirect-gain and isolated-gain passive solar systems where different mass sizing rules apply; non-passive solar buildings.

## R-125: Lumber Moisture Content: Kiln-Dried Specification

**Statement:** Structural framing lumber must be specified as kiln-dried (KD) with a maximum moisture content of 19% (KD19) at time of delivery. **Intent:** Allen & Iano establish that moisture content is among the most consequential properties of wood for construction performance. Green or wet lumber exceeding 19% MC is common in dimensional lumber shipments and causes measurable post-installation problems.

## R-126: Pressure Treatment for Hazardous Contact Conditions

**Statement:** Wood members in any of the following conditions must be pressure-treated to the applicable AWPA standard retention level or replaced with a naturally durable species: (1) in contact with concrete or masonry, (2) in contact with soil or within 6 inches of finished grade, (3) exposed to water or weather, (4) in enclosed crawl spaces with inadequate ventilation.

**Intent:** Allen & Iano document that untreated wood in contact with concrete, soil, or water is the leading cause of decay and termite damage in light-frame construction. **Exceptions:** Interior wood framing at interior concrete podium conditions where concrete is protected by a capillary break and moisture exposure is not anticipated.

## R-127: Wood Shrinkage Detailing at Cross-Grain Connections

**Statement:** Structural details involving cross-grain wood connections must account for differential shrinkage: tangential shrinkage ~8% and radial shrinkage ~4% from green to oven-dry (approximately half these values from green to air-dry). **Intent:** Cross-grain shrinkage is a fundamental wood property that causes differential movement at connections as lumber dries. **Exceptions:** Engineered lumber (LVL, PSL, LSL) and CLT have significantly lower shrinkage than sawn lumber; apply standard EWP shrinkage values where these products are used.

## R-129: Mass Timber Minimum Member Dimensions for Code Fire Resistance

**Statement:** Unprotected (exposed) mass timber members must meet the minimum char-layer dimensions specified by IBC Chapter 23 and AWC Technical Report 10 to achieve the required fire-resistance rating. Post minimum cross-section: 6x8 in nominal (heavy timber) or species/product-specific char-adjusted dimension for 1-2 hour mass timber ratings. Any proposed reduction in member dimension — through machining, notching, or architectural shaping — must be verified against char-reduced residual capacity.

**Intent:** Allen & Iano establish that large-dimension wood achieves fire resistance not through non-combustibility but through sacrificial char formation that insulates the structural core. The char rate for solid sawn and glulam is approximately 1.5 in/hr; for CLT approximately 1.8 in/hr. If minimum residual dimensions are not maintained, the member loses structural capacity under fire conditions before the required rating is met.

**Detection:** Has the structural engineer confirmed that exposed mass timber member dimensions satisfy the char-reduced residual capacity check? Are all proposed notches, holes, or profile changes evaluated against fire performance? Does the project narrative identify the required fire-resistance rating and confirm the selected mass timber product meets it?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Use IBC Table 2304.10.1 minimum dimensions for heavy timber classification; for mass timber ratings (1 hr, 2 hr), apply AWC TR10 char-reduced capacity method. Avoid notching at mid-span of beams without supplemental fire protection (intumescent coating, gypsum enclosure) at the notch location.

**Exceptions:** Mass timber members protected by a compliant enclosure (gypsum sheathing) where fire-resistance is achieved by assembly rather than exposed char.

## R-130: Mass Timber Floor Assembly Acoustic Mitigation

**Statement:** Mass timber floor assemblies (CLT, NLT, DLT) must include an acoustic upgrade layer to meet IBC minimum IIC 50 / STC 50 for occupancy separations. Bare mass timber panels perform significantly below this threshold (IIC ~20-30, STC ~35-40 for untopped panels). The floor assembly must specify a topping strategy (concrete topping, floating wood, resilient underlayment) with tested assembly IIC/STC values meeting the code minimums.

**Intent:** Allen & Iano note that acoustic performance is a known limitation of mass timber relative to concrete and steel floor systems. Unlike concrete, wood is a low-density material with inherent resonance characteristics that amplify impact and airborne sound transmission. Without deliberate acoustic detailing, mass timber buildings routinely fail post-occupancy acoustic surveys and require expensive retrofits.

**Detection:** Does the project assembly schedule include an acoustic upgrade layer for all mass timber occupancy-separation floors? Consult acoustic consultant for residential over commercial or hotel over office programs. Coordinate acoustic penetrations with structural drawings.

**Exceptions:** Single-occupancy, single-tenancy buildings where IBC separation requirements do not apply (e.g., single-family residential with no party separation).

## R-131: CLT vs One-Way Mass Timber Panel System Selection

**Statement:** Cross-laminated timber (CLT) shall be selected when two-way spanning, dimensional stability under biaxial load, or large-format panel continuity is required. Nail-laminated (NLT) and dowel-laminated (DLT) timber panels are one-way systems and must be oriented with their span direction perpendicular to the supporting beams; they shall not be used as two-way spanning elements. System selection must be documented in the structural basis-of-design narrative.

**Intent:** Allen & Iano distinguish CLT's alternating-grain lamination — which provides two-way plate action — from NLT/DLT, which act as one-way assemblies analogous to a joist system. Misapplying a one-way panel in a two-way span condition causes overloading along the weak axis, excessive deflection, and potential failure. Conversely, specifying CLT where a more economical NLT/DLT would suffice adds cost unnecessarily.

**Detection:** Has the structural engineer identified the spanning direction of all mass timber panel zones? Are NLT/DLT panels shown with a span-direction indicator on structural drawings? Does the framing plan support the required panel span direction?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** For irregular bays or column-free zones requiring two-way plate action, specify CLT with documented span table compliance. For rectangular bays with parallel beams, NLT or DLT is acceptable and more economical; orient span perpendicular to supporting beams and verify L/360 or L/480 deflection limits.

**Exceptions:** Hybrid systems where CLT is used as a diaphragm and NLT/DLT is used for non-structural panel fill — document separately.

**Source:** Fundamentals_of_Building_Construction_Materials_an..._----_(Chapter_4_Heavy_Timber_and_Mass_Timber_Construction).pdf (Ch. 4 — Mass Timber / CLT, NLT, DLT system types)

## R-132: Glulam Long-Span Precamber Specification

**Statement:** Glulam beams with spans exceeding 20 feet must specify precamber equal to the anticipated dead load deflection (typically L/360 for roof, L/480 for floor). The precamber value must appear on the structural drawing, and the camber direction (upward) must be indicated on shop drawings and confirmed by the inspector at delivery.

**Intent:** Allen & Iano note that glulam's primary structural advantage is efficient bending over long spans. Without precamber, dead load deflection causes permanent sag that creates visible slope perception, ponding risk on low-slope roofs, and accumulated live-load deflection exceeding code limits. Precamber is manufactured into the member at the glue-lamination plant; it cannot be added after fabrication.

**Detection:** Do glulam beam drawings include a precamber value and direction notation? Are camber requirements identified in the structural specification section for glulam? Is the delivery inspection checklist for engineered wood products on the submittal register?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Calculate dead load deflection under full dead load using delta = 5wL^4/384EI; specify precamber at 1.0-1.2x dead load deflection. For roof beams on low-slope roofs, add additional camber to account for ponding risk. Coordinate precamber with finish ceiling datum.

**Exceptions:** Glulam beams <=20 ft span where dead load deflection is minor; glulam columns; glulam in truss configurations where node geometry controls.

## R-134: Rainscreen Drained-and-Ventilated Cavity at Exterior Cladding

**Statement:** All exterior cladding on wood-frame construction must be installed over a continuous water-resistive barrier (WRB) with a minimum 3/8-in drained and back-ventilated air gap (rainscreen cavity) between the WRB and the back face of the cladding. The cavity must be open at the bottom (weep/drain strip) and vented at the top. Cladding installed in direct contact with the WRB without a drainage plane is not compliant.

**Intent:** Allen & Iano establish the rainscreen principle as the primary moisture-durability strategy for wood-frame buildings. Even a properly installed cladding allows moisture penetration through joints, weeps, and capillary action. The cavity allows this moisture to drain and the assembly to dry by back-ventilation. Buildings without drainage planes show moisture accumulation at the sheathing face leading to mold, rot, and sheathing delamination within 5-10 years.

**Detection:** Does the wall section detail show a drainage mat, furring strip, or other spacer creating a minimum 3/8-in cavity between WRB and cladding? Is there a weep/drain provision at the base of each cladding field? Is the cavity open at the top for back-ventilation? Is this detail consistent across all cladding types?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Use minimum 1x3 or 3/4-in pressure-treated furring strips over WRB; install drainage mat (Home Slicker or equivalent) as an alternative. Ensure weep screed at base of stucco and fiber cement runs full width. Coordinate drainage plane with flashing at windows — drainage must exit above all window head flashings.

**Exceptions:** Masonry veneer with integral cavity and weep ties; drainage EIFS systems with their own cavity requirement.

**Source:** Fundamentals_of_Building_Construction_Materials_an..._----_(Chapter_6_Exterior_Finishes_for_Wood_Light_Frame_Construction).pdf (Ch. 6 — Protection from the Weather / rainscreen principle)

## R-135: Flashing at All Cladding Penetrations and Transitions

## R-136: Continuous Air Barrier at All Assembly Transitions

**Statement:** The building air barrier system must be continuous across all assembly transitions — wall-to-roof, wall-to-floor-diaphragm, wall-to-foundation, and wall-to-wall corners — with no gaps or unaddressed terminations. A single narrative note "continuous air barrier throughout" without drawn transitions is insufficient.

**Intent:** Allen & Iano and ASHRAE 90.1 establish that air barrier systems routinely fail not at field conditions but at transitions — where different trades, different materials, and different installation sequences create discontinuities. Without detail-level continuity instructions, inspectors and contractors cannot verify compliance.

## R-137: Climate-Appropriate Vapor Retarder Placement

**Statement:** Vapor retarder placement must match the project climate zone: (a) Climate zones 5-8 (cold/very cold): vapor retarder on the interior (warm-in-winter) face of insulation, Class II (kraft paper, 1-mil polyethylene); (b) Climate zones 1-2 (hot-humid): vapor retarder on the exterior face or vapor barrier sheathing; (c) Mixed/marine (zones 3-4): vapor-permeable Class III assembly with drying capacity in both directions. Vapor retarder must not be installed on both sides of the insulation simultaneously.

**Intent:** Allen & Iano document that incorrect vapor retarder placement drives wetting and drying potential in the wrong direction, trapping moisture in the insulation or sheathing plane. Cold-climate buildings with an exterior vapor barrier trap winter condensation against the sheathing. Hot-humid buildings with an interior vapor barrier trap summer condensate driven inward by solar-pumped moisture. Both failure modes lead to mold and structural decay.

**Detection:** Is the vapor retarder location identified on wall and roof section details? Does the specification section for vapor retarders reference the IECC or ASHRAE 160 climate zone requirements? Is the project climate zone explicitly stated on the code compliance sheet?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Reference ASHRAE 90.1 Table 5.8.7.1 (or IBC Table 1405.3) for vapor retarder class by climate zone. For mixed-climate projects, run a Glaser method or WUFI analysis to confirm drying potential. For renovations adding insulation to existing walls, verify existing vapor retarder is on the correct side before proceeding.

**Exceptions:** Continuous exterior insulation systems where the exterior R-value exceeds 50% of total — use hygrothermal analysis rather than simplified placement rules.

## R-138: Continuous Exterior Insulation to Eliminate Framing Thermal Bridging

**Statement:** Wood-frame wall assemblies relying solely on batt insulation between studs must include a thermal bridging correction factor in the energy model: effective R-value of a 2x6 stud wall with R-21 batts is approximately R-14 to R-16 (20-33% reduction due to framing). When project energy targets require exceeding code minimum effective R-values, continuous exterior rigid insulation (CI) must be added; CI thickness must be documented and coordinated with window/door rough openings, flashing depths, and structural ledger connections.

**Intent:** Allen & Iano document that thermal bridging through framing members reduces wall R-value by 20-40% relative to the nominal cavity insulation R-value. Buildings designed to code on nominal R-values may fail whole-assembly performance. Adding CI also raises sheathing temperature above the dew point, synergizing with moisture management (R-134, R-137).

**Detection:** Does the energy model use effective (parallel-path or isothermal-plane) R-values rather than nominal cavity values? Is a thermal bridging correction factor applied? If the project must achieve more than code minimum R-value, is CI specified with thickness documented on wall sections?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Apply parallel-path correction using ASHRAE 90.1 Appendix A framing factors (wood: 0.97 multiplier on cavity R). For target effective R-20 in Climate Zone 5 with 2x6 framing: specify minimum R-5 CI + R-21 batt. Coordinate window rough-opening extension jambs and head/sill flashing with added wall thickness. Check ledger attachment: through-CI ledger attachment requires structural screws (Ledger-LOK or equivalent).

**Exceptions:** SIP walls, ICF walls, or double-stud walls with interior insulation fill — these assemblies have inherently low bridging and do not require a separate CI layer.

## R-139: Structural System Type Selection Within SD Span Ranges

**Statement:** During schematic design, structural system type must be matched to the governing span range: wood light frame (joists/rafters) for spans up to approximately 20-25 ft; wood post-and-beam / mass timber for spans up to approximately 40 ft; steel or concrete framing for spans of 40-100 ft; long-span systems (trusses, space frames, cable structures) for spans exceeding 100 ft. Selecting a system outside its appropriate range without structural justification is a design error detectable at SD review.

**Intent:** Allen & Iano's Studio Companion establishes these span ranges to prevent costly structural redesigns between SD and DD. A system selected at SD that is found to be inadequate for the required span requires full structural reconception — typically 2-4 weeks of redesign time. **Detection:** Does the SD structural basis-of-design document identify the governing span and the system selected? Is the selected system within its applicable span range? If atypical spans are required, is there a structural engineer consultation note in the record?

**Exceptions:** Purpose-designed structural elements (long-span transfer beams, cantilever conditions) where the system choice is driven by program requirements — these require early SE involvement by definition.

## R-140: Beam Depth Rules of Thumb for Preliminary Sizing

**Statement:** For preliminary structural sizing at SD, beam depths shall be estimated using these span-to-depth ratios: steel W-shape = span/20; wood beam (sawn or glulam) = span/15; reinforced concrete beam = span/12 to span/15; post-tensioned concrete flat plate = span/40 (one-way) to span/45 (two-way). Beam depths deviating more than 25% from these ratios must be justified in the structural basis-of-design before proceeding to DD.

**Intent:** Allen & Iano document these ratios as standard preliminary-design tools for avoiding coordination conflicts between structural depth and floor-to-floor height budgets. A beam significantly deeper than the rule of thumb signals either a heavily loaded condition, long span, or potential cost overrun that should be resolved at SD rather than discovered during DD structural modeling.

**Detection:** Have preliminary beam depths been calculated and checked against the floor-to-floor height budget? Are structural depth assumptions consistent between section cuts and the structural narrative? Is the depth budget (structural + MEP zone + finish) shown on a floor-to-floor height diagram?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Create a floor-to-floor height diagram at SD: slab + beam depth + MEP zone + ceiling/finish = total floor-to-floor. If beam depth plus MEP exceeds the allocated structural zone, evaluate alternatives (wide shallow beams, castellated beams, post-tensioned flat plates). Coordinate with MEP engineer on duct size assumptions at SD.

**Exceptions:** Transfer beams, long-span atrium beams, and heavily concentrated-load conditions routinely exceed rule-of-thumb depths — these must be called out as atypical and sized by SE at SD.

## R-141: Floor-to-Floor Height Minimum for Structural, MEP, and Finish Assembly

**Statement:** Commercial building floor-to-floor heights must budget: (a) floor structure + beam depth (from R-140 rule of thumb), (b) MEP zone: minimum 18 in for open-plan commercial ductwork and piping; 24-30 in for hospital or data-center densities, (c) finished ceiling clearance including fire sprinkler line. Resulting floor-to-floor minimum is typically 12-14 ft for standard commercial office; 10-11 ft is feasible only with flat-plate concrete and minimal MEP. A floor-to-floor height below 10 ft in commercial construction requires SE and MEP engineer confirmation.

**Intent:** Allen & Iano establish floor-to-floor height as one of the most consequential early decisions: once set, it determines floor count, building height, and relationship to zoning limits. Under-allocated floor-to-floor heights are discovered in DD when the MEP engineer cannot fit ductwork, requiring either a floor-to-floor revision (triggers all floor plan and facade re-drawing) or a value-engineering compromise to MEP distribution.

**Detection:** Does the SD section show floor-to-floor height with an explicit breakdown of structural, MEP, and finish components? Is the floor-to-floor budget consistent across all typical floors? If a zoning height limit applies, has the total building height been calculated using the documented floor-to-floor?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Use 13 ft as a default starting assumption for commercial office; adjust up for complex MEP programs. Confirm with MEP engineer that the MEP zone budget is sufficient for the project mechanical strategy before setting floor-to-floor height as a design parameter.

**Exceptions:** Residential construction where 9-10 ft floor-to-floor heights are standard and MEP is simpler; single-story buildings where the floor-to-floor constraint does not apply.

**Source:** The_Architects_Studio_Companion_Rules_of_Thumb_for_Preliminary_Design_5th_Edition_by_Edward_Allen_Joseph_Iano.pdf (Building systems / floor-to-floor height)

**Confidence:** High

## R-142: Lateral System Height Limits by System Type

**Statement:** Lateral load-resisting system selection must be matched to building height: wood or concrete shear walls are appropriate to approximately 10 stories; reinforced concrete or steel moment frames to approximately 25 stories; steel braced frames (concentric or eccentric) to approximately 40 stories; outrigger, belt-truss, and tube systems for taller structures. Use of a lower-performance lateral system above its range requires structural peer review and is a risk flag at SD.

**Intent:** Allen & Iano document that as building height increases, base overturning moment grows with the square of height and wind/seismic forces require progressively stiffer and more ductile systems. A shear-wall-only building at 15 stories is not inherently impossible but requires unusually thick walls, significant plan area commitment, and may not be economical.

**Detection:** Does the SD structural basis-of-design identify the lateral system type? Is the building height (stories) within the applicable range? If the building is at or above the rule-of-thumb limit for the system, is there a structural engineer narrative explaining the justification?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** At SD, confirm the lateral system with the structural engineer and document the selection basis. For mixed-use towers (podium + residential), the lateral system may change at the podium level — document the transition. For seismic design categories D-F, lateral system selection is also constrained by ASCE 7 Table 12.2-1.

**Exceptions:** Special structural systems (shear walls with outriggers, hybrid diagrid-frame) may extend height limits — these require SE peer review.

## R-143: Gross-to-Net Efficiency Targets by Occupancy Type

**Statement:** Preliminary building programs must include an explicit gross-to-net (GTN) efficiency factor appropriate to occupancy: commercial office 75-85%; residential (multi-family) 80-90%; healthcare (hospital inpatient) 55-65%; educational (K-12) 65-75%; hotel 65-70%. Programs with GTN below these ranges signal circulation, structure, or service-core over-allocation that should be resolved at SD before client confirmation of rentable area.

**Intent:** Allen & Iano document these efficiency factors as standard benchmarks for design accountability. Gross-to-net loss is the mechanism by which programmatic area inflates to gross building area. Early-stage clients often confuse net program area with gross building area, leading to either underbuilt programs or overbuilt (and over-budget) buildings.

**Detection:** Does the SD program document include both net program area and gross building area with an explicit GTN factor? Is the GTN factor within the range for the occupancy type? Are the major area consumers (core, circulation, structure) itemized in the program?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Create a two-column program with (1) net program area by room/space and (2) a gross multiplier (1/GTN) to obtain gross building area. Flag any space type with an unusual GTN contribution for owner review. For mixed-use buildings, apply separate GTN factors to each use and sum to obtain total gross area.

**Exceptions:** Single-function utilitarian buildings (warehouses, parking structures) where GTN norms do not apply; transportation terminals where low GTN is intentional.

**Source:** The_Architects_Studio_Companion_Rules_of_Thumb_for_Preliminary_Design_5th_Edition_by_Edward_Allen_Joseph_Iano.pdf (Building systems / gross-to-net efficiency)

**Confidence:** High

## R-144: Mechanical Room Area Allowance in Program

**Statement:** The building program must allocate dedicated mechanical room area equal to 3-8% of gross building area. The lower bound (3-4%) applies to simple single-use buildings with limited equipment (small office, residential). The upper bound (6-8%) applies to hospitals, laboratories, and data centers. Programs that allocate less than 2% — or omit mechanical area entirely — are incomplete and will require redesign when the mechanical engineer provides an equipment schedule.

**Intent:** Allen & Iano document that mechanical room area is a consistent source of late-stage redesign conflict: the room is omitted or undersized in early programming because it is non-revenue space, and the conflict is discovered only when the mechanical engineer provides an equipment room layout, requiring relocation of revenue-generating uses.

**Detection:** Does the SD program include a mechanical room line item with an area allocation? Is the allocation within the 3-8% range? Has the mechanical engineer reviewed and confirmed the allocated room area is sufficient?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** At SD program completion, add a mechanical room line item at 5% of gross area as a default. Request preliminary mechanical room sizing from the MEP engineer before finalizing the program. If the project uses rooftop mechanical, the mechanical room area can be reduced but not eliminated (must accommodate electrical switchgear, MDF, and domestic water equipment).

**Exceptions:** Buildings where all mechanical equipment is rooftop or exterior-mounted and no indoor equipment room is required (some retail pad buildings, outdoor event facilities).

**Source:** The_Architects_Studio_Companion_Rules_of_Thumb_for_Preliminary_Design_5th_Edition_by_Edward_Allen_Joseph_Iano.pdf (Building systems / mechanical room area allowance)

**Confidence:** High

## R-145: Elevator Count Rule of Thumb for Commercial Office

**Statement:** Commercial office buildings must provide a minimum of one elevator per 45,000-50,000 sq ft of gross building area, rounded up. A 200,000 sq ft building requires a minimum of 4-5 elevators. The elevator count must appear in the SD program and be coordinated with core dimensions before setting floor plates. Elevator count below this threshold requires elevator consultant confirmation of acceptable wait times (maximum 30-second average interval).

**Intent:** Allen & Iano establish elevator count as a standard core-planning determinant that, when under-provisioned, is discovered at DD when the elevator consultant confirms unacceptable handling capacity — at which point adding a core bay requires significant floor plan revision.

**Detection:** Does the SD core diagram show the elevator count and shaft dimensions? Is the elevator count consistent with the gross area rule of thumb? Has an elevator consultant been identified for buildings over 100,000 sq ft?

**Fix / Generation Guidance:** Calculate elevator count: round(gross_sf / 47,500, up). For mixed-use buildings, calculate residential and office elevator banks separately (residential: 1 elevator per 50-60 units). Confirm shaft dimensions with elevator manufacturer at SD (standard passenger cab requires approximately 7x9 ft hoistway). Add elevator consultant to the project team budget for buildings over 100,000 sf.

**Exceptions:** Low-rise buildings (3 stories or fewer) where stair-only egress is feasible and elevators may be discretionary; parking structures.

**Source:** The_Architects_Studio_Companion_Rules_of_Thumb_for_Preliminary_Design_5th_Edition_by_Edward_Allen_Joseph_Iano.pdf (Building systems / elevator count)

**Confidence:** High
