## Summary

Defines biophilic design's 3 dimensions (nature in the space, natural analogues, nature of the space) and 14 attributes. Provides operational vocabulary for integrating living systems into architecture.

## Principles

- Biophilic design requires ongoing human-nature relationships, not mere exposure
- Direct nature contact (plants, water, animals) produces measurable stress reduction and attention restoration
- Indirect nature: natural materials, naturalistic shapes, fire, and images of nature carry biophilic value
- Prospect, refuge, mystery, peril — spatial configurations with evolutionary resonance
- Thermal and airflow variability (not constant HVAC uniformity) mimics outdoor experience and is preferred
- Dynamic and diffuse light — skylights, changing shadows — outperforms static artificial lighting for wellbeing
- Seasonal and weather legibility from indoors: connection with natural systems
- Fractal geometries common in nature are preferred over pure rectilinear grids
- Biophilic ROI: 6-12% productivity gains and 15-35% illness reduction documented in office/hospital studies
- Integration across scales: site to building to room to detail, not siloed biophilic gestures
