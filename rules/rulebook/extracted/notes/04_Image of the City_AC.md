## Summary

Lynch's five elements (paths, edges, districts, nodes, landmarks) form the cognitive map framework for planners and architects. High imageability creates legible, memorable, navigable environments.

## Principles

- 5 elements: paths (routes), edges (linear breaks), districts (zones), nodes (strategic points), landmarks (external reference)
- Imageability = quality that evokes a strong, clear mental image — a measurable design goal
- Legibility: environment readable as a coherent pattern supports wayfinding and reduces anxiety
- Good paths have continuity, directional quality, scale differentiation, and facade variety
- Landmarks must be visually prominent AND positionally unique — height alone is insufficient
- Edges can be seams (connecting) or barriers (dividing) — design intent should be explicit
- Nodes require spatial definition and clarity of use, not just visual ornamentation
- Districts need perceptual homogeneity: common texture, color, material, or use pattern
- Cognitive maps vary by mobility — design for the least mobile user to capture all users
- Structure + identity + meaning all compose the mental image; all three must be designed
