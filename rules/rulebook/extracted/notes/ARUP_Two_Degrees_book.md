## Summary

Industry briefing on 2-degree climate targets and their implications for building design, infrastructure resilience, and professional responsibility.

## Principles

- Buildings account for ~40% of global energy use and ~30% of CO2 emissions
- 2-degree pathway requires near-zero-carbon buildings by 2050: passive-first, then efficient systems, then renewables
- Embodied carbon is increasingly the dominant lifetime impact for well-insulated buildings
- Design must address both adaptation AND mitigation
- Flood resilience: ground-floor program choices and threshold heights are now risk management decisions
- Heat resilience: urban heat island mitigation through cool roofs, tree canopy, permeable paving
- Passive survivability: buildings should remain habitable 72hrs without power
- Net zero carbon requires commissioning, measurement, and reporting — intent is insufficient without verification
- Professional responsibility: architects have an ethical duty to advocate for low-carbon design
- Lifecycle thinking: material selection must include carbon cost, not just first cost
