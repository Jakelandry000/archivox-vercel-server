## Summary

Evidence-based design for healthcare facilities. Reviews research linking spatial features to patient outcomes and staff performance.

## Principles

- Single-occupancy patient rooms reduce infection rates, improve sleep, and increase patient control
- Access to daylight and nature views reduces length of stay and analgesic use
- Noise is a clinical quality issue in healthcare, not just a comfort concern
- Decentralized nursing stations improve response time and reduce patient falls
- Family accommodation space improves patient emotional outcomes and care coordination
- Wayfinding legibility in hospitals directly impacts patient anxiety and staff efficiency
- Flexibility: healthcare spaces must accommodate rapid program changes — loose-fit design required
- Infection control: surface material selection is a design decision with clinical consequences
- Staff fatigue reduced by direct visual supervision, shorter travel paths, and natural light at workstations
- Evidence-based design requires post-occupancy evaluation to close the loop between intent and outcomes
