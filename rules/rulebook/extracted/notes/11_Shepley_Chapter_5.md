## Summary

Design features that support therapeutic goals, staff safety, and resident autonomy in behavioral health and rehabilitation environments.

## Principles

- Balance safety (anti-ligature, supervision) with dignity and autonomy in behavioral health design
- De-institutionalization aesthetic: residential scale, warm materials, natural light — reduces stigma and improves outcomes
- Outdoor access significantly improves mood and reduces aggression in inpatient behavioral health settings
- Activity differentiation: separate spaces for active and quiet reduce conflicts between therapeutic activities
- Indirect supervision (soft visual contact from corridors) is less stigmatizing than panopticon nursing stations
- Anti-ligature fixtures and tamper-resistant hardware need not look institutional — specify with dignity
- Reduced sensory overwhelm: controlled acoustic design, muted color, and regulated artificial light reduce agitation
- Cluster units of 6-12 patients outperform large wards in behavioral health clinical outcomes
- Clear thresholds between staff-only, patient, and shared spaces reduce friction and unsafe mixing
- Post-occupancy evaluation is critical in behavioral health — design assumptions must be tested against outcomes
