## Summary

The profession's primary dimensional and technical reference, organized by CSI element (A: Substructure, B: Shell, C: Interiors, D: Services, E: Equipment, F: Special Construction, G: Sitework). Covers foundations, structural systems, envelope, MEP coordination, accessibility, and code compliance with graphic standards. Key quantitative data: floor system span ranges, soil-bearing conditions, seismic design categories, stair geometry, ADA clearances, and climate-specific wall section families. Paired with The Architects' Studio Companion for preliminary design; AGS provides the detailed design and documentation layer.

## Principles

- Geotechnical report is required before foundation selection — cohesive vs. cohesionless soils, consolidation behavior, and overconsolidation state all affect allowable bearing pressure and settlement (p. 4–5, Substructure §Soils).
- Foundation type selection sequence: spread footings for competent soils; mat/raft for weak or variable soils; piles or drilled piers for inadequate bearing capacity at grade or high seismic demand (p. 10–15).
- Seismic design: building period (0.1–4.0 s) must not match predominant ground-motion period (0.5–1.0 s) to avoid resonance; seismic design category from IBC governs detailing requirements and must be confirmed at project initiation (p. 8–9, §1.10–1.12).
- Floor system selection by span: wood joists ≤18 ft; wood I-joists/trusses 12–30 ft; structural steel joist 16–130 ft; concrete one-way slab 10–20 ft (more with post-tensioning); two-way slab 10–30 ft. All preliminary — engineer confirmation required (p. 30, §2.13 Floor Structure Assemblies table).
- Post-tensioning is the threshold strategy for concrete spans > 40 ft (p. 29).
- Acoustic performance of floor systems: wood joist systems have poor airborne and impact sound isolation; concrete/structural systems provide Fair–Good ratings. Healthcare and multi-family require STC/IIC testing of assemblies (p. 31, §2.13 table, "Comparative Resistance to Sound Transmission").
- Hot-arid climate wall sections: use vented-skin masonry or vented-skin wall with radiant barrier; avoid exposed wood; avoid thermal bridges (cantilevered slabs); radiant barriers in roof cavity required (p. 7, §1.6–1.8).
- Climate-specific vapor retarder placement: in hot humid climates, vapor retarder on exterior side of insulation to prevent condensation on cooling season; in cold climates, interior side. Wrong placement causes chronic moisture damage (p. 7, §1.6).
- ADA: 5 ft × 5 ft turning radius at every activity node; 36 in. minimum corridor; 32 in. min door clear opening; 18 in. latch-side clearance pull side, 12 in. push side.
- Stair geometry: riser 7–7.75 in. (residential IRC), ≤7 in. (commercial IBC); tread ≥11 in.; riser + tread = 17–18 in. rule of thumb; nosing 0.75–1.25 in. projection.
- Modular coordination: 4-in. (100 mm) planning grid aligns structure, MEP rough-in, and finish systems — deviations require custom dimensions and increase cost.
- Drawings and specifications are complementary — conflicts resolved only by formal RFI; drawings govern dimension, specs govern quality.
