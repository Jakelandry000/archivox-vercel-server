## Summary

Wood as structural and finish material: species, grades, seasoning, light frame construction, engineered lumber, and connections.

## Principles

- Specify wood by species, grade, and treatment (e.g., No.2 Douglas Fir, KD-HT)
- Moisture content critical: lumber at >19% MC will shrink and cause finish/structural problems; specify KD
- Platform framing is the dominant US residential construction method
- Engineered lumber (LVL, PSL, LSL, I-joists) allows longer spans with more dimensional predictability
- Fire performance: char rate for mass timber ~1.5in/hour; heavy timber can achieve fire ratings by dimension alone
- Wood connections: nails, screws, bolts, metal connectors each have shear and tension allowable loads per NDS
- Shrinkage: tangential ~8%, radial ~4% drying from green to dry — detail for movement at connections
- Pressure-treat wood in contact with concrete, soil, or water; use naturally durable species where exposed
- Shear walls: plywood/OSB sheathing with nailing schedule provides lateral resistance
- CLT (cross-laminated timber) enables mass timber multi-story construction with good fire and seismic performance
